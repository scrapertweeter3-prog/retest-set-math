Regression you can bill for

A prompt suite does not rot all at once. It rots in the corners: a system prompt tweak here, a model swap there, and last quarter's green checkmarks quietly mean nothing. Most teams absorb that by re-running everything, which is the expensive answer, because the bill scales with suite size rather than with what actually changed. The retest set is the number worth managing, and the clustering approach written up in the PastAGI guide to shrinking retest sets is the cheap way to shrink it: group prompts by what they share, test one per group, escalate only on failure.

Put dollars on a mid-size suite. Fourteen hundred prompts at four minutes each is five thousand six hundred minutes, about ninety-three hours per full regression pass. At a blended eighty-five dollars an hour for the engineer or contractor watching it, one pass costs roughly seventy-nine hundred dollars. Four passes a year is about thirty-two thousand. That is most of a salary spent re-confirming prompts that never changed.

Clustering changes the shape of that bill. Say the fourteen hundred prompts fall into four hundred twenty clusters by shared context, an average of about three prompts per cluster. Testing one sampled prompt per cluster is four hundred twenty checks, twenty-eight hours, about twenty-four hundred dollars a pass. That is a seventy percent cut, and the cut is honest about its own risk: a sampled failure makes its whole cluster suspect, so you re-run the cluster, about two extra prompts per failure. Four sampled failures re-run eight or nine prompts, noise money. The guarantee floor is exact: if every sampled prompt fails, the re-runs walk the total to fourteen hundred checks, the same as the full pass. Never more than brute force; far less the moment most clusters come back green.

The one-time cost is the mapping. Reading fourteen hundred prompts and clustering them by dependency is about twenty hours of work, seventeen hundred dollars once. Against roughly twenty-two thousand a year saved, it pays for itself inside a month, and every pass after that runs at the discounted price. The blunt test that keeps this from becoming astrology: a suite of genuinely unrelated prompts clusters into fourteen hundred singletons and the method collapses back to the full run. Cluster on shared context, not shared topic, or do not cluster at all.
